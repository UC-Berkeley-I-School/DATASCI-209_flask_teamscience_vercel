Team Research NIH Grants

Summary

Over the course of 2025, more than 7,800 research grants were terminated or frozen (specifically 5,844 at the NIH and 1,996 at the NSF with roughly 2,600 grants still unresolved as of January 2026). This represents $1.4 billion in unspent funding. The NIH issued 24% fewer new grants in 2025 compared to the previous ten-year average, and the NSF issued 25% fewer. These cuts initially targeted grants containing topics like “vaccine hesitancy”, “misinformation”, “infectious diseases” and research on people from under-represented groups. This expanded to affect research on Alzheimer's, cancer, drug discovery, and global health across institutions nationwide.

What's informative about this view: Grant termination rates vary across NIH research areas, and there does not appear to be a strong relationship between research area size and termination rate. Many of the highest termination rates appear in public health-related areas, including rural health, health disparities, minority health, infectious diseases, and vaccine-related research.

What could be improved about this view: These visualizations show which research areas have higher termination rates, but they do not explain why those grants were terminated. Other factors, such as funding institute, grant size, project length, or overlap between research categories, could also influence the observed termination rates.
